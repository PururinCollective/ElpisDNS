Choose Your Gun
Somewhere between your keyboard and the rest of the world, a machine is asked a very simple question: where is this? Whoever answers that question owns your internet. They can log it. They can sell it. They can decide that today, for reasons nobody wrote down, this particular door is closed. Most people never find out who is answering. You are about to.

Everything on this table is loaded and encrypted end to end — DNS over HTTPS, DNS over TLS — so the answer arrives sealed and the question never travels in the open. Pick the one that fits the job: a local resolver for speed, an international one for the doors that keep mysteriously closing, DNS64/NAT64 if your network has already moved on from IPv4. The prettiest gun in the drawer is rarely the one that gets you home.

And we wrote the resolver
The endpoints we operate do not hand the real work to somebody else's software. Behind them sits ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver — ours, written from nothing in portable C99, no dependencies, one static binary, open source down to the crypto. It is the second resolver in the world to validate ML-DSA-44, the post-quantum DNSSEC signature, after Cloudflare. How it fits behind the filtering is on the mission page; the source is on GitHub, and running your own is encouraged rather than merely permitted.
ML-DSA-44
Second in the world
The post-quantum signature from FIPS 204, checked here and at Cloudflare and almost nowhere else. Signatures gathered today are what somebody forges later; this is the answer given early.
C99 + SIMD
Faster than generic
Hand-written AVX2, SSE2 and NEON kernels picked at startup from what the processor reports, so the crypto and the cache lookups run at the speed of the machine rather than the speed of portable C.
OPEN & PORTABLE
Take it and go
One static binary, no shared libraries, Linux, the BSDs and macOS. Build it for your own CPU, put it behind your own AdGuard Home, and stop needing us at all.
Read it before you trust it
The source, the DNSSEC notes and the reasons the hot paths look the way they do — including the parts that are deliberately missing. A resolver you cannot read is a promise you cannot check.

House rules
01
The question is nobody's business
A list of the names you look up is a confession you never agreed to make. Encrypted transport keeps that confession between you and the resolver.
02
Censorship is just bad routing
A block list is somebody's opinion, enforced with a hammer, on a network that was designed to route around exactly that.
03
Monitoring arrives dressed as convenience
The free resolver handed to you by default is rarely free. Somewhere, a column with your name on it is being sorted.
04
Your curiosity is not merchandise
ΕΛΠΙΣ exists to answer the question and then forget it was asked. That is the whole arrangement. No ledger, no profile, no sales pitch.
Running your own resolver?
Every entry on this page comes out of a single file: dns.json. Copy a block, change the values, open a pull request. No build step, no framework, no meeting. CONTRIBUTING.md has the whole ritual, and it fits on one screen.
