ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver

Our own recursive DNS resolver. It finds every answer by asking the internet's root servers directly, so there's no Google, Cloudflare or anyone else in the middle to see your lookups. Free, open source, and yours to use.

Asks the source

Resolves from the root servers down. There's no upstream provider, so nobody else gets a copy of your lookups.

Checks every signature

DNSSEC with RSA, ECDSA, Ed25519 and post-quantum ML-DSA-44 — the second resolver in the world to check it, after Cloudflare.

Quick

0.6 ms median from cache, with hand-tuned code for your CPU: AVX2, SSE2 or NEON, picked at start-up.

Small and open

Portable C99 with no dependencies: one static binary of about 1.4 MB. GPL-2.0 licensed.

How it fits together

ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver does the quiet part: finding answers and checking them. A filter such as AdGuard Home sits in front and takes care of blocklists and encrypted connections.

  1. Your devices Phones, laptops, TVs — anything on the network.
  2. AdGuard Home or Pi-hole Blocklists, per-device rules, DoH and DoT.
  3. ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver Recursion, caching and DNSSEC.
  4. Root, TLD and authoritative servers Where every answer really lives.

That is exactly how ΕΛΠΙΣ DNS runs. We keep several resolvers side by side: AdGuard spreads the load across them and carries on if one is away for an upgrade.

Public resolver addresses

Free for anyone. These are unfiltered on purpose: they answer everything and leave the blocking to your own AdGuard Home, Pi-hole or similar. For filtered DNS on a phone or laptop, get an address from the home page instead.

IPv6 is direct. Straight to the resolver on port 53, with nothing translating in between. Use it when you can.
IPv4 skips port 53 on purpose. Ports 5301–5306 keep scanners and abuse away. Any decent DNS app lets you set a port.

Use it with AdGuard Home or Pi-hole

Already filtering at home? Keep your blocklists and swap the upstream. Instead of sending every lookup on to Google or Cloudflare, your filter asks ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver.

Three steps

  1. Pick your app and your network, then copy the list.
  2. Paste it where the caption says, replacing Google, Cloudflare or your ISP.
  3. In AdGuard Home, set Upstream mode to Load-balancing, press Test upstreams, then Apply.

Using encrypted hostnames anywhere else in AdGuard? Put the IPv6 addresses in Bootstrap DNS servers too, so it can find them on a cold start.

Check it's working

Don't take our word for it — two quick ways to see for yourself.

From a terminal

Ask a resolver directly. An ad flag in the reply means the DNSSEC signatures checked out. 

dig @2402:4e20::babe example.com +dnssec
dig @151.158.198.49 -p 5301 example.com +dnssec

From a browser

Once your filter uses ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver, open dnscheck.tools. You should see DNSSEC and IPv6 light up, and your resolver named as Perfect Network in Semenyih — not your internet provider.
