The arrangement
Why ΕΛΠΙΣ exists
Every website you have ever opened began with somebody being asked a question on your behalf: where is this? It is a small question. It is also a complete record of your curiosity, handed over in the clear, several hundred times a day, to whoever your network happened to name.

ΕΛΠΙΣ is that question answered by people who want nothing from it. What follows is the whole arrangement — what we block, what we refuse to keep, who we ask when we do not know, and how to check every word of it without taking our word for anything.
THE QUESTION
Is nobody's business
A list of the names you look up is a confession you never agreed to make. Encrypted transport keeps it between you and the resolver, and the resolver has no use for it afterwards.
THE DOOR
Stays where it is
We block advertising, tracking and malware, and we say so in writing. We do not quietly decide which opinions, countries or libraries you are allowed to reach. A resolver with politics is a censor with better manners.
THE LEDGER
Does not exist
There is no profile with your name on it, because there is nothing to put in it and nobody to sell it to. The lights are kept on by operators and sponsors, not by your browsing history.
THE CHAIN
Ends with us
We do not hand your query to Google or Cloudflare and call the result privacy. We ask the root servers ourselves, on our own hardware, on our own AS.
THE PROOF
Is yours to demand
Every claim on this page can be tested from your own machine in under a minute, with tools we do not control. Trust that cannot be checked is just advertising.
THE LIMIT
Is stated plainly
Encrypted DNS hides which names you ask for. It is not a VPN, it does not hide the addresses you then connect to, and it will not make you anonymous. Anyone promising otherwise is selling something.

The Blacklist
Every name on it earned its place. Ads, trackers, malware, phishing kits, and the quiet little domains that exist for no reason other than to follow you home — the resolver simply declines to tell you where they live. Nothing to install, nothing to configure: it is already running on the endpoint you picked on the front page.

No Middlemen
Most privacy resolvers are a polite fiction. Your question arrives sealed, and is then handed straight to Google or Cloudflare in the clear. One padlock, two custodians, and the second one keeps records.

ΕΛΠΙΣ answers it itself. The query goes to the root servers, down through the TLD, to the authoritative server that actually holds the answer — asked from our own hardware, on our own AS, over our own transit. Total privacy, total independence. There is nobody upstream to sell you to, because there is nobody upstream.
RECURSIVE
We ask, nobody asks for us
Root, then TLD, then the authoritative name server. No forwarding to a household name, and no third party quietly assembling the other half of your day.
IPv6 FIRST
The address space that is not rationed
Native v6 from the resolver out, AAAA ahead of A, and DNS64/NAT64 for networks that have finished with IPv4 entirely.
EDNS(0)
Room to answer properly
Responses larger than 512 bytes, DNS cookies, and the headroom that signatures need. The modern internet does not fit in a packet designed in 1987.
DNSSEC
Signatures checked, not admired
A tampered answer fails instead of arriving with a shrug. Ask for a domain whose chain is broken — wrong signature, expired, missing — and you get nothing at all, which is the only honest reply. RSA, ECDSA P-256 and P-384, Ed25519 — and ML-DSA-44, which almost nobody checks yet.
ECH
The handshake stays shut
HTTPS and SVCB records served intact, ech parameter and all, so your browser can encrypt its Client Hello and stop announcing which site it wants before it has even asked.
VERIFY
Take nobody's word for it
Ours least of all. dnscheck.tools reports who actually answered you, from which network, and what they bothered to validate.
Our own resolver
Answering the question yourself means running something that can. So we wrote one. ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver is a recursive resolver in portable C99 — the crypto, the event loop and the wire format all in one tree, no dependencies, one static binary you copy to a machine and run. It is open source, because a resolver you cannot read is a promise you cannot check.

It is also the second resolver in the world to validate ML-DSA-44, the post-quantum DNSSEC signature from FIPS 204, after Cloudflare. Not because a quantum computer is at your door this evening — because the signatures collected today are the ones somebody intends to forge later, and a resolver that waits for the standards body to finish is a resolver that arrives after the fact.
It does not face you directly, and it is not trying to. AdGuard Home keeps the encrypted transports, the block lists and the per-client rules; Elpis sits behind it and does the part nobody sees — recursion from the root, caching, and every signature on the way.

That is the shape, not the scoreboard. The two b00b recursors below still answer from PowerDNS while we convert them one at a time; everything else is Elpis already. A resolver swap nobody notices is the only kind worth doing.
ML-DSA-44
Second in the world
FIPS 204 verification written from the specification, with the draft's worked example — key tag, DS digest and a real RRSIG — checked on every single build. 65 and 87 are in there too.
SIMD
Machine speed, not generic speed
Hand-written AVX2, SSE2 and NEON kernels for the hot paths, chosen at startup from what the CPU actually reports. The crypto runs faster than a portable C compiler would ever manage on its own.
C99
Portable on purpose
Strict C99 and POSIX.1-2008. epoll on Linux, kqueue on the BSDs and macOS, poll everywhere else. One static binary, no shared libraries, nothing to install beside it but its config.
NO UPSTREAM
Root, TLD, authoritative
It resolves rather than forwards, so there is no second custodian to trust and none to audit. The chain ends at the servers that actually hold the answer.
TESTED
Every vector path, byte for byte
The suite checks each SIMD kernel against the scalar one and needs no network to do it, so the only difference between the fast path and the plain one is how long it took.
YOURS TOO
Build it for your own CPU
make static and it is done; point -march at your own processor for the last few percent. Put it behind your own AdGuard Home and stop needing us at all.
Read it before you trust it
The source, the DNSSEC notes, the cache sizing and the reasons the hot paths look the way they do — all of it is in the open, including the parts that are deliberately missing.
ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver
Recursive resolvers — IPv6
The recursors themselves, on plain port 53, doing exactly one thing: resolving. No block lists, no rewrites, no NXDOMAIN where an answer ought to be. Whatever the authoritative server says is what comes back, whether we approve of it or not.

Which makes these the wrong thing to hand a laptop, and the right thing to put behind a box that already does its own filtering. Running AdGuard Home, Pi-hole, Blocky, Technitium or anything of that family? Set these as your upstream. You keep your lists, your logs and your rules; we do the recursion, and your household stops handing every query it makes to Google or Cloudflare on the way out. They work in the bootstrap field too — the one that has to resolve an encrypted endpoint's hostname before the encryption can begin.

These are ΕΛΠΙΣ Resolver answering, and anyone is welcome on them. IPv6 is preferred and it is not sentiment: the v6 path goes straight out of our AS without a translator in the middle of it.
IPv6 only, and unfiltered on purpose. Bootstrap DNS servers
